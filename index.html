Investor questions and suggested answers

Why would a distributor pay?

Our hypothesis is that better demand visibility and confirmed replenishment can improve delivery planning and retention. We will ask the buyer to quantify its problem and sign at a stated price. That value remains unproven.

Why can customers not use the sensor supplier’s app?

They can. CGI must justify its charge through local installation, distributor coordination, exception handling and reporting. If the existing app meets the need economically, we should partner or narrow our service.

Do you already have customers or live sensors?

This proposal does not establish secured contracts or deployed sensors. The first gate requires a paid agreement, site access and a technically workable data path. Describe any newer verified progress precisely.

Does US$20,000 fund expansion?

It funds the proposed 90-day validation ceiling. The illustrative growth scenario requires about US$34,856 total funding, including a US$3,000 reserve. Expansion starts only after the pilot gate and requires separate approval.

What investment return are you offering?

The preferred initial instrument is a staged grant or corporate pilot sponsorship. Equity valuation, investor rights and return expectations require separate diligence. No return is promised.

How strong is the climate case?

LPG monitoring alone does not prove lower emissions. We will measure delivery fuel and operating changes against a baseline. Carbon revenue remains zero in the base case. Cooling and biogas need separate technical and commercial gates.

What is the biggest risk?

A customer may not pay enough to cover installation and ongoing support. We will stop or redesign if the price, data path or delivery process fails. Unspent funding should remain subject to the agreement.

What should we agree today?

An owner for sponsor diligence, introductions to a distributor and qualified technical partner, and a review within 14 days. Confirm the contracting entity, platform IP rights, insurance and partner responsibilities before funds move.

An LPG distributor pays for visibility across participating commercial customers, supported by a per-asset service charge and a fixed portfolio fee. The distributor may absorb or transparently pass on the charge under its own agreement.

Alternative: a hotel or restaurant group contracts directly. These are alternative payer structures; do not count the same service twice.

Pricing proposal and customer discovery

Test US$20–35 per active asset per month, a US$150–400 monthly portfolio fee and US$100–200 onboarding per installed asset. These are commercial hypotheses, not market quotations. The scenario default is US$25 + US$250 portfolio fee + US$150 onboarding. All figures exclude tax. Interview at least 10 businesses and two distributors. Ask for refill frequency, recent emergency-delivery records, downtime costs, current monitoring methods and a purchase decision at a stated price. Seek a signed paid pilot, not only expressions of interest.

Bottom-up expansion hypothesis: 5 sites × 2 assets = 10 pilot assets; later 25 sites × 4 assets = 100; later 125 sites × 4 assets = 500. These are recruitment scenarios, not verified market size. Connectivity may instead be bundled or gateway-based; replace these allowances with the chosen vendor quotation. Actual replacements will cause uneven cash expenditure.

Fixed monthly allowance, US$1,800: founder/project coordination US$900; shared field-partner retainer US$400; cloud/platform US$150; insurance/professional provision US$100; sales/customer acquisition US$150; administration US$100. These are lean budget allocations, not employment offers or established market rates. Variable remote support covers incremental asset tickets; the field retainer covers standing service capacity. Confirm that neither supplier invoices nor staff time are double-counted.

At 250–500 assets, a dedicated team may be necessary. Increase the fixed-cost slider to stress this requirement; the model does not automatically add staff. Onboarding charges recover part of installation cash, and are excluded from recurring break-even.

Open model formulas, accounting basis and limitations

Assets: month 1 = initial assets; later months = previous assets × (1 − monthly churn) + gross new assets (new additions begin only in the selected first expansion month, default M4 after the 90-day gate), capped at capacity. At the cap, new installations can replace churn. Fractional assets represent expected portfolio averages. New assets earn a full month of fees: this simplifying assumption is optimistic for mid-month installs.

Revenue: active assets × monthly asset fee + one fixed portfolio fee when assets > 0. Onboarding income = gross installations × onboarding fee. Fee is charged once per new installation, including replacement customers after churn; no renewal onboarding is assumed.

Recurring operating profit: recurring revenue − active assets × variable monthly cost − fixed monthly overhead. This is a management contribution measure before depreciation, tax, financing and owner distributions, not audited EBITDA. Installation surplus is presented separately.

Cash: collected recurring fees (shifted by 0/1/2 months) + immediate onboarding receipts − installation outlays − recurring operating costs. Month 0: selected funding less startup development. Funding is not income. Delayed receivables remain uncollected at forecast end; there is no terminal recovery in the chart. Bad debt is not separately modelled: use stress prices/costs and validate credit terms.

Break-even: ceiling[max(0, fixed overhead − portfolio fee) ÷ (asset fee − variable cost)]. If asset contribution is non-positive, report no scalable asset break-even. Liquidity: max(0, chosen reserve − minimum cash including month 0). Payback: commercial cumulative cash, excluding funding, recovers all startup/installation outflows. No exit valuation, carbon income, gas-sales margin, transaction fees or equipment loan is assumed.

Installed cost is a cash outlay, not a depreciation policy. Fixed overhead is a lean shared-team allowance and must include realistic founder compensation, insurance, field support and professional services before investment. No tax, VAT, financing charges, inflation, seasonal demand or hardware salvage is modelled. Reserve is a liquidity floor, not an expense. Model inputs stay in US$; currency switching changes display only at EC$2.70/US$1, excluding bank spreads. Changes persist only during the open session; export a CSV to keep a record.

06 / IMPACT & PLATFORM REUSE

Earn the climate claim.

Operational value comes first

Level visibility and planned deliveries may improve continuity. They do not automatically reduce emissions or establish climate adaptation. LPG is a fossil fuel; higher gas sales are not counted as climate impact.

Baseline route distance, litres consumed, emergency trips and delivered quantities. Compare similar routes and delivery volumes; document weather, customer mix and other changes. Use a cited fuel emission factor only after reliable activity data exist.

Inclusion is measured

Track voluntary participation of women-owned/led businesses, affordability, interruptions and user-reported time saved. Record a baseline and use consistent definitions. Offer SMS or dispatcher-assisted access where smartphones or data are limiting.

Collect only necessary information with consent. Do not promise gender impact merely from counting users.
